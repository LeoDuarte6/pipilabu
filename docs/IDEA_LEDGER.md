## NOW — current v0.9.x repair gate: handoff feel plus one color choice

Build and judge only this loop:

- The player remains a normal Roblox avatar in classic third-person for the current gates. A custom, color-variable worker-dog avatar is a later option and must not block Peepilabu or the core loop.
- Pick up, visibly carry, and serve one requested apple at a time to smaller Pipi Labu customers. The only active choices are `Ruby` and `Honeygold`; the first two orders teach Ruby before Honeygold appears.
- Arrange the eight-customer queue parallel to the counter so customers advance left-to-right, not front-to-back. Keep only the next small Doge serviceable.
- Make the handoff itself intrinsically satisfying through physical motion, contact, sound, animation, and feedback.
- Let the served Doge visibly receive and hold the apple for a readable beat before leaving; the handoff should not look like the prop disappears on contact.
- On successful contact, draw from a no-immediate-repeat shuffle bag of high-pitched baby-customer `Peepilabu` reactions. The handoff owns the strongest sound; UI remains quieter.
- Keep the compact HUD in the upper-left but translate it below Roblox's chat/menu/CoreGui safe area. Remove the disliked `GOOD FRUIT` heading and use short, literal prototype copy until Leo and Jon author the final language.
- Restore/retain a grounded base and readable shop footprint so the kiosk never appears to float.
- Run one complete eight-customer first shift with no replacement spawns. Ask whether matching Ruby/Honeygold creates satisfying attention or merely adds walking. The handoff still has to be the sensory peak, and the wrong apple must remain in hand so the game teaches without confiscating effort.
- The current Physics Lab comparison is one continuous object journey rather than three duplicate props: pluck one dirty apple, carry it into deep responsive water, physically pour soap, scrub the same apple clean, then drop it into a hollow lift-lid crate where it falls and locks into a grid slot. Comfort, elastic response, and repeat desire matter more than urgency or difficulty.
- Give the shift a concrete, positive stake through a physical payday rather than arbitrary tragedy or punitive urgency. Performance should change the amount; clearing the full line should earn a visible bonus. The shopkeeper hands over a world object, the avatar visibly holds it, and the balance creates desire for the next mechanic. v0.9.9 tests this with base pay, per-customer pay, and a clear bonus while persistence and spending remain closed.

## NEXT — accepted one-variable gates after v0.9

Promote one layer at a time, with a human gate after each change:

- Leo and Jon accepted `Gate P1 — One Ruby, One Springy Branch` as the first sourcing/physicality test. Its implementation is integrated behind `Enabled=false` and `StudioOnly=true`; the current v0.9.x gate blocks only enabling or promoting P1, never independent production. P1 is exactly one attached Ruby on one springy branch in one isolated source: grab, pull or twist against immediately visible tension, pop free, catch or recover, carry, and serve through the unchanged `Paw_R` handoff. Weight, inertia, wobble, persistent motion, and readable/recoverable comedy are the target. The client may predict presentation; the server owns detach, inventory, and serve truth. The complete accepted contract is in `docs/design/GATE_P1_ONE_RUBY_ONE_SPRINGY_BRANCH.md`.
- `Gate P2 — One Dirty Apple, One Wash/Polish Station` follows P1 rather than competing with it. One dirty apple should become clean through actual hand motion, with responsive local ripples/particles, dirt removal, tactile sound, and haptics. Transfer the linked satisfying-simulation reference's immediate response, persistence, inertia, and expressive variation without attempting networked full-fluid simulation.
- Add colored-apple order matching, then impatient customers who visibly leave. Gate: the added choice creates satisfying attention rather than extra walking or confusion.
- Add potion/treatment dipping and mage/barista-style crafting only after color matching works. Gate: one color × one treatment × one request multiplies the proven action cleanly.
- Add shop upgrades and progression only when repeated service creates a clear desire for capacity, expression, or mastery.
- The first desired spending targets are better apple access and authored recipes. Treat these as physical gameplay unlocks, not a menu-only currency sink. Prove that collecting cash itself feels satisfying before building the shop.
- Give friends adjacent shops and allow visiting, carrying supplies, clearing overflow, and generous help. Gate: helping happens naturally and feels worthwhile; generosity/helping may later become required for progression only if this test is humane rather than coercive.
- After one local shop passes its gates, test multiplayer as one shop per player. Add durable account/save-state progression only after the multiplayer ownership model and reset behavior are explicit and independently verified.
- Preserve the open product-design questions as one future gate: how Jon joins Leo's session, what progression belongs to the account versus the current shift/shop, what completing or "beating" the game means, and how later line growth feels cozy and satisfying rather than punitive. Do not solve these by adding random dispatchers or urgency systems to level one.
- Run visual-design passes before deep mechanic expansion. Replace the standard Roblox avatar with a custom playable Doge/Cheems/Shiba direction; prove silhouette, scale, camera, and movement before committing to a final rig.
- Prototype a single pre-handoff talk action: the player/shopkeeper says a short character callout such as `What the dog doing?`, the front Doge visibly answers, and the exchange may earn XP or reputation. Gate: it must create anticipation or relationship before the proven handoff, not become compulsory dialogue busywork.
- Peepilabu is the anchor owner NPC and the current hero-character priority: large, cute, mildly stressed, active behind the counter, and eventually capable of giving tasks or quests. Gate: the character must make the shop feel alive without obscuring the service loop or becoming a static mascot.

## LATER — preserved, gated expansion

- Expand sourcing only after a one-branch pluck is proven: compact orchard trees, the ordinary market, and a periodic farmer's-market route should each offer a distinct physical verb, timing pressure, or risk rather than functioning as reskinned menus.
- Add leaf removal, washing, soap/water agitation, cloth polishing, slicing, and caramel or honey coating one action at a time. Preserve the accidental rice-apple idea as an authored novelty candidate rather than an ambient accident promoted directly into the game. Every preparation candidate needs its own uncoached verb gate and must strengthen the serve loop instead of delaying it with chores.
- Preserve fried chicken as a later recipe-family candidate: physically batter, cook, and serve it to Doge customers. It is not part of the apple-first gate and should be attempted only after one paid recipe unlock and one preparation verb prove that broader food does not dilute the game's identity.
- Preserve the [Square Race reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y0J_sYKBf24) as abstract direction for a later **living market**. Its transferable quality is continuous unresolved motion and visible board-state change: many simple autonomous actors collide, separate, recombine, change totals, and meet periodic hazards while the state remains legible. Do not copy its graphics or turn Pipi Labu into a passive simulation.
- After the core physical verbs pass, test visible customer lines, delivery carts, apple-source traffic, guards/robbers, and small disruptions as bounded living-market systems. They may create session depth and return variety only when players can understand what changed at a glance. They must never replace tactile sourcing, preparation, carry, and service verbs or become screen-noise.
- Build retention in three earned layers: first prove voluntary replay across a satisfying 2–5 minute pluck/carry/serve loop; then target 20–30 minute session depth through source variation, preparation variation, and living-market events; later create day/week return reasons through rotating market conditions, recipes and rarities, social shops/helping, mastery, and authored events.
- Instrument voluntary replay, time-to-first-serve, abandonment, source choice, recipe completion, and—only when real return cohorts exist—D1, D7, and D28 retention. Use these as evidence for iteration; never claim an algorithm can guarantee retention.
- Build rows of shops and streets only after one shop and neighbor-helping are readable and fun.
- Explore a weekly competitive farmer's-market inventory scramble, including possible playful punching, only after core service and multiplayer helping work. Competition must not erase the game's humane generosity.
- Add controlled disruptions—a robber Doge, fire, rotten apples, and other surprises—to create an “anything could happen” feeling. Each disruption must reuse actions players already understand and be introduced one at a time.
- Prototype one rare VIP interruption only after ordinary color matching works: a suited/fedora customer arrives under a brief lighting/spotlight beat and requests one Mythic apple. The event may temporarily redirect the player to a distant mountain or source, but it must reuse known carry/serve actions and must not become a cutscene that replaces play.
- Test economic character moments one at a time: customers who pay coins, an occasional poor customer who cannot pay, reputation consequences, and robberies. These are future moral/systemic experiments, not permission to complicate Gate A or punish generosity prematurely.
- Expand shop upgrades, progression, inventory, products, and weekly preparation only when they sharpen the proven handoff instead of replacing it with menus.
- Develop a future story and deeper meaning after the action loop earns it.

## Presentation direction

- Design around classic third-person, but permit optional first-person rather than locking the camera out of it.
- Support console and multiplayer as real product targets, sequencing them through explicit gates rather than building them speculatively.
- The canonical Pipi Labu appearance is Leo's exact supplied market-video family: one enormous natural Shiba seller behind the counter and a long line of tiny low quadrupedal Shiba/loaf customers. Upright top ears should not really be visible; they are folded, swallowed by the crown/fur, or out of the silhouette. The earlier upright-ear concept sheets are staging references only, and the procedural Blender v4/side-ear model is not visually acceptable. Fur, face anatomy, eye placement, muzzle shaping, paws, scale contrast, and soft mass must survive the Blender-to-Roblox path rather than collapsing into primitive blocks. No half-measure candidate may be promoted merely because it imports. The player remains a normal Roblox avatar for the current gates.
- Pursue unusually satisfying, Overwatch-like audio and feedback: readable anticipation, impact, reward, and character response without noisy excess.
- Aim for a humane Animal Crossing/Wind Waker-ish visual spirit: warm, authored, readable, playful, and emotionally generous.
- Consider an eventual custom playable worker-dog rig with Among Us-like color variation only after the Peepilabu owner and customer cast pass their art/animation gates. It must not read as a default Roblox asset or generic AI output.
- Treat primitive/current models as test infrastructure, not the final visual identity.
- Treat `assets/reference/peepilabu-market-reference.mp4` as the visual grammar reference: one oversized shopkeeper, a clean line of tiny customers, fruit as the focal prop, readable reach/contact beats, and strong size contrast. Re-author the final assets rather than imitating video artifacts.
- Expand toward a compact Japanese market/city village: narrow streets, rows of shops, shelving, awnings, and dense readable commerce. This is the map direction after the one-shop composition is proven, not a reason to build the full town during Gate A.
- Treat map work as mechanic work whenever space changes travel time, order visibility, queue reading, helping, risk, surprise, or discovery. Pure decoration stays in the isolated art lane; a new location needs a player verb and a named uncoached test before integration.
- Graybox an orchard only after plucking is fun. Use large, medium, and small landmarks, reveal/occlusion, reach, sightlines, and route choice to support the verb; do not imitate a Breath of the Wild map or build a large world before the compact route earns it.
- Begin interaction sound design with the verb: branch strain, leaf rustle, stem pop, apple wobble, impact, roll, water movement, cloth squeak, and the final customer reaction. A restrained Pipi Labu motif may develop in parallel, but a full or adaptive soundtrack waits until shift pacing and distinct locations are stable.
- The warm medallion HUD direction in `assets/concepts/pipi_labu_hud_direction_v1.png` is approved for native implementation: three small top-left state medallions, compact circular order/patience language, and an unmistakable `SHIFT CLEAR -> NEXT SHIFT` state. Rebuild it responsively from Roblox UI primitives; do not ship the concept image as a baked overlay.
- Phase-offset and randomize customer idle animation timing. A queue of synchronized idles is never acceptable presentation.
- Give visible structural objects honest collision, including roofs, counters, shelves, trunks, and solid branches when shown as physical. Do not apply collision blindly to non-solid VFX, particles, prompts, or markers.
- Voice selection, the interaction sound palette, UI concept approval, and the final character look are explicit Leo/Jon creative gates. Automated or overnight work may prepare bounded candidates but cannot approve them.
- Shopkeeper and customer dialogue should use bottom-screen subtitle composition rather than distant floating text as its primary reading plane. Later, give each character family a restrained, original, bit-crushed syllable/bleep voice grammar inspired by the readability of Animal Crossing, The Sims, and Undertale without copying their sounds.
- Jon's pear-cat reference is a future harvest/event lead: a fruit-shaped round creature with tiny side-folded ears, strong squash, expressive face, and basket/tree context. It may become a rare orchard customer or sourcing surprise, but it must remain visually distinct from the Pipi Labu dog cast and cannot replace the owner or player by accident.

## Meaning

Let biblical themes of service, stewardship, generosity, good fruit, honest work, and care for neighbors emerge through play and consequence. Do not use blunt religious exposition. A future story may deepen this meaning after the core actions are joyful.

## Method

Use Nintendo/Kojima-style action-first, player-action-centered development:

1. Identify the smallest player action worth repeating.
2. Build one bounded playable change.
3. Observe Leo and Jon without coaching.
4. Record the result honestly.
5. Promote exactly one new variable only after the current gate passes.

Add the Epic-derived candidate and iteration discipline in `docs/EPIC_WORKFLOW.md`.

- When Leo and Jon are together, work exclusively on gameplay: control, interaction feel, challenge, spatial readability, multiplayer behavior, telemetry, and immediate playtest decisions.
- When Leo and Jon are away, Hermes/Luna may advance visual development, character sheets, models, props, environments, materials, animation studies, and supporting documentation in isolated reversible lanes.
- Hermes consumes the curated idea ledger and accepted design specs, not raw voice transcripts. The Design Room filters ambient conversation, preserves decisions, and hands Hermes bounded tickets so overnight work cannot promote stray dictation into the game.
- Every visual integration ends at a named gameplay check-in. That check-in blocks only promotion/default enablement of the exact dependent integration; independent code, art, tools, maps, audio preparation, tests, multiplayer/save architecture, and disabled prototypes continue immediately.
- The handoff/contact with the object remains the current sensory center: the apple must be easy to see while carried, land cleanly, remain in the customer's possession, and trigger the strongest animation/audio response.

Do not treat ambient jokes, politics, drugs, unrelated chatter, or accidental dictation as requirements. When Leo and Jon are AFK, record human questions in `docs/codex/PLAYTEST_QUEUE.md` and keep bounded deterministic work moving.
