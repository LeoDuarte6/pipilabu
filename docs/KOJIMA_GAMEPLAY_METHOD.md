# Kojima Method Applied to Doge Apple Shop

The useful lesson is not “make it cinematic.” It is to control what the player knows, let actions carry the theme, create anticipation before pressure arrives, and kill or redesign a core concept that playtesting cannot validate.

## Practical rules

1. **Constrain the first contract.** The first three orders contain one red crate, one carried apple, and one valid recipient. No potions, timers, alternate products, or irrelevant prompts compete with learning the verb.
2. **Keep a familiar verb as the entrance to depth.** “Carry the requested item to the customer” remains permanent. Color, treatment, condition, route, and generosity multiply around it instead of replacing the controls.
3. **Show the next problem approaching.** A special customer should appear at the end of the street five to ten seconds before reaching the counter. Silhouette, gait, and a short audio motif create anticipation without a UI ambush.
4. **Make the theme an action.** Service, stewardship, and generosity are expressed by physically carrying stock, helping another shop, and receiving a thank-you. Test Goodwill without an economic reward first.
5. **Alternate tension and relief.** A future rhythm may use 45–60 seconds of serving pressure followed by 8–12 seconds of applause, restock, musical resolution, and one funny Doge animation.
6. **Treat the core as falsifiable.** Measure first pickup, first serve, invalid interactions, second-run improvement, and voluntary continuation. If the serve verb fails twice after feedback/input fixes, redesign the verb or route before adding content.

## Ranked applications

1. Controlled First Contract — current Gate A.
2. Approaching Customer Foreshadowing — only after Gate A.
3. Rush/Relief Audio State — pair with the 90-second Dandori test.
4. Goodwill Action Prototype — pair with the neighbor-help multiplayer gate.
5. Kojima/Nintendo kill gate — no colors, potions, robberies, or farmer's market if the core still fails.
